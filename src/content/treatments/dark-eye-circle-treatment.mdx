<div class="answer">
<p><strong>The best dark eye circle treatment depends on the cause.</strong> Pigment, blood vessels, hollowing and thin under-eye skin may need different treatment directions, and many patients have more than one cause at the same time.</p>
</div>

<ul class="clean">
<li>Dark circles can be pigment-based, vascular, structural, thin-skin related or mixed.</li>
<li>Mixed dark circles are common, so one treatment may not solve everything.</li>
<li>Laser may help pigment, but it does not correct hollow shadows.</li>
<li>Skin boosters and polynucleotides may help selected cases of thin, tired or crepey under-eye skin.</li>

## What are the main types of dark eye circles?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>The main dark eye circle types are pigment, vascular, structural or shadow, thin-skin related and mixed dark circles.</strong> Identifying the type helps avoid choosing the wrong treatment.</p>
</div>

### Pigment dark circles

Pigment-type dark circles usually look brown or darker than the surrounding skin. They may be linked to genetics, sun exposure, rubbing, inflammation, allergies or skin tone.

### Vascular dark circles

Vascular dark circles may look bluish, purplish or grey because blood vessels show through thin under-eye skin. This can become more obvious when the skin is thin, dehydrated or tired-looking.

### Structural or hollow dark circles

Structural dark circles happen when the under-eye area becomes hollow. The hollow creates a shadow, especially around the tear trough. This type may not improve with pigment laser because the problem is not only colour.

### Thin or tired under-eye skin

Some patients have under-eye skin that looks crepey, dry, thin or dull. In this case, skin quality support may be part of the treatment plan.

### Mixed dark circles

Many patients have a mixed type. For example, someone may have brown pigment, thin skin and a hollow shadow together. Mixed dark circles usually need a staged or combination plan.

## What causes dark eye circles?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>Dark eye circles can be caused by genetics, pigmentation, visible blood vessels, thin skin, hollowing, ageing, allergies, rubbing, sun exposure, dehydration or lifestyle factors.</strong> The cause may be different from one person to another.</p>
</div>

Common contributing factors include:

<ul class="clean">
<li>Genetic tendency</li>
<li>Brown pigmentation or melanin buildup</li>
<li>Visible blood vessels under thin skin</li>
<li>Loss of under-eye volume</li>
<li>Deeper tear trough shadow</li>
<li>Dry or crepey under-eye skin</li>
<li>Sun exposure</li>
<li>Eye rubbing or allergies</li>
<li>Ageing-related skin and fat changes</li>
<li>Sleep, stress and lifestyle factors</li>
</ul>

Sleep can make dark circles look worse, but many people still have dark circles even when they sleep enough. This is because the cause may be structural, pigment-related or genetic.

## How does hollowing create dark circles?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>Hollowing creates dark circles by forming a shadow under the eyes.</strong> When the tear trough becomes deeper, light does not reflect evenly, so the area looks darker even without extra pigment.</p>
</div>

This is why some people feel that eye creams or brightening products do not help. If the darkness is mainly from a hollow shadow, the treatment direction needs to focus on structure and support rather than pigment removal alone.

## Can pico laser help dark eye circles?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>Pico laser may help pigment-type dark circles by targeting excess pigment.</strong> It is not meant to correct hollow shadows caused by volume loss.</p>
</div>

For Asian skin, the treatment must be planned carefully because aggressive treatment can increase the risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. The doctor will assess your skin type, pigment pattern and sensitivity before deciding whether laser is suitable.

## Can skin boosters help dark eye circles?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>Skin boosters may help selected patients with thin, tired, dry or crepey under-eye skin.</strong> They focus on skin quality rather than filling a hollow shadow.</p>
</div>

A skin booster may be considered when the under-eye area looks dehydrated, dull, tired or thin. It may support hydration and skin texture, but it is not a replacement for pigment laser or structural correction when those are needed.

## Can polynucleotide treatment help under-eye skin?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>Polynucleotide treatments may help selected patients by supporting skin repair, hydration, elasticity and overall skin quality.</strong> They may be considered when thin or tired under-eye skin contributes to the appearance of dark circles.</p>
</div>

Examples of polynucleotide-based treatment categories include regenerative skin boosters such as Rejuran, Plenhyage or similar options, depending on clinic availability and doctor recommendation.

## What if dark circles are caused by hollowing?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>If dark circles are mainly caused by hollowing, the treatment direction may need to address structure or volume rather than pigment alone.</strong> Laser or brightening skincare may not fix a shadow caused by a deeper tear trough.</p>
</div>

Some patients may benefit from improving skin quality around the hollow, while others may need a more structural plan. This should be assessed carefully because the under-eye area is delicate.

## Is dark eye circle treatment safe for Asian skin?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>Dark eye circle treatment can be safe for Asian skin when the treatment choice, energy settings, injection plan and aftercare are suitable.</strong> The under-eye area is delicate, so assessment and conservative planning are important.</p>
</div>

Asian and darker skin types may be more prone to post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation after irritation or aggressive treatment. This is why gentle planning, sun protection and realistic expectations matter.

## What results can you expect?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>Dark eye circle treatment aims to improve the appearance of under-eye darkness, not guarantee complete removal.</strong> Results depend on the cause, treatment type, skin response, ageing pattern and aftercare.</p>
</div>

Some patients may notice improvement in brightness, skin texture or tired appearance. Others may need staged treatment because pigment, thin skin and hollow shadows respond differently.

A realistic goal is to soften the dark circle appearance and make the under-eye area look less tired while keeping the result natural.
