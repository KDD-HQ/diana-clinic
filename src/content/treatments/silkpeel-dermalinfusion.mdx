## What is SilkPeel Dermalinfusion?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>SilkPeel Dermalinfusion is a non-invasive resurfacing facial that exfoliates the skin with a diamond-encrusted tip, extracts loosened debris under vacuum, and infuses a condition-specific serum into the freshly abraded surface — all in a single pass.</strong> Because the skin is wet and the serum is delivered under controlled pneumatic pressure at the moment the outer barrier is thinnest, the active is placed onto skin that is more receptive to it than intact, dry skin would be.</p>
</div>

At Klinik Dr Diana, SilkPeel Dermalinfusion is used as a skin-conditioning and maintenance treatment for congested, dull and unevenly toned skin, and as an adjunct alongside medical acne care, peels and laser. Sessions are performed under doctor supervision at our Bandar Baru Bangi (Selangor) and Bandar Prima Senawang (Negeri Sembilan) branches.

## What you need to know

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>SilkPeel Dermalinfusion works on the surface layers of the skin, so it is well matched to texture, congestion, dullness and superficial discolouration — and poorly matched to deep scarring or volume loss.</strong> Choosing it for the right indication is what determines whether the result feels worthwhile.</p>
</div>

<ul class="clean">
<li>The mechanism is mechanical abrasion plus vacuum extraction plus simultaneous serum delivery — not a chemical peel and not a laser.</li>
<li>The abrasive surface is a diamond-encrusted tip, available in different grit grades, so exfoliation depth is adjustable rather than fixed.</li>
<li>The dermatologic literature describes the procedure as appropriate across all Fitzpatrick skin types, which is directly relevant to Malaysian skin.</li>
<li>Serum choice is what personalises the session — hydrating, brightening and clarifying formulations target different concerns.</li>
<li>Improvement in glow and hydration is often immediate; changes in tone and congestion accumulate over a course.</li>
<li>It does not treat depressed acne scars, and it does not cure melasma or acne.</li>
</ul>

## Is SilkPeel the same as Dermalinfusion and DiamondGlow?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>SilkPeel, Dermalinfusion and DiamondGlow all refer to the same underlying technology under different names.</strong> SilkPeel was the original device name from Envy Medical, Dermalinfusion is the name of the patented exfoliate-extract-infuse process, and DiamondGlow is the current brand name following the technology's acquisition by Allergan Aesthetics.</p>
</div>

This matters because patients research the same treatment under three different names and assume they are three different procedures. In Malaysia, the name SilkPeel Dermalinfusion remains the one most commonly used by clinics and by patients searching in both English and Bahasa Malaysia.

## How does SilkPeel Dermalinfusion work?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>SilkPeel Dermalinfusion works by combining three actions that normally happen separately: abrasion, extraction and infusion.</strong> A closed-loop handpiece draws the skin up against a diamond-encrusted tip using vacuum, the tip removes part of the stratum corneum, the vacuum carries away loosened corneocytes and sebum, and a serum flows onto the treated surface under pneumatic pressure in the same movement.</p>
</div>

The clinical logic is straightforward. The stratum corneum is the main barrier limiting how much of a topical active reaches the living epidermis. Removing part of that barrier and delivering the active at the same moment — while the surface is still wet and open — places the ingredient in more favourable conditions than applying a cream to intact skin at home.

The serum also serves a mechanical purpose. It lubricates the interface between the diamond tip and the skin, which is why the treatment is often described as wet dermabrasion and why it tends to feel less scratchy than dry crystal microdermabrasion.

## What can SilkPeel Dermalinfusion help with?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>SilkPeel Dermalinfusion is used for congested pores, rough or dull texture, dehydrated skin, superficial uneven tone including post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, and pre-event skin conditioning.</strong> Reported dermatologic indications for the device include dry skin, acne, hyperpigmentation and rosacea.</p>
</div>

<ul class="clean">
<li><strong>Congestion and comedones</strong> — blackheads and clogged pores, particularly on the nose, chin and forehead.</li>
<li><strong>Dull, rough texture</strong> — accumulated stratum corneum that makes skin look flat and makes makeup sit unevenly.</li>
<li><strong>Dehydration</strong> — skin that feels tight despite being oily, a common pattern in air-conditioned indoor work.</li>
<li><strong>Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation</strong> — the flat brown marks left after acne resolves, which are pigment rather than scar.</li>
<li><strong>Pre-event conditioning</strong> — a session booked a few days before an event, chosen precisely because it does not produce peeling.</li>
</ul>

### What it will not do

Being clear about the ceiling of a treatment is more useful than listing its benefits.

<ul class="clean">
<li>It will not correct depressed acne scars — boxcar, rolling and ice-pick scars sit in the dermis and require subcision, TCA CROSS or fractional laser.</li>
<li>It will not cure melasma. Melasma is a chronic, relapsing pigmentary condition managed over time, and aggressive surface treatment can aggravate it.</li>
<li>It will not replace medical acne treatment. Where acne is inflammatory or hormonally driven, topical or oral therapy remains the foundation.</li>
<li>It will not lift, tighten or restore volume, because it does not act on collagen at depth.</li>
<li>It will not produce permanent change on its own. Benefit is maintained with repeat sessions and home care.</li>
</ul>

## What happens during a SilkPeel session at Klinik Dr Diana?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>A SilkPeel Dermalinfusion session at Klinik Dr Diana takes approximately 45 minutes from cleansing to sunscreen, with the device portion occupying roughly 20 to 25 minutes.</strong> Every session begins with a doctor-led skin assessment, because the serum and the device settings are chosen from that assessment rather than from a fixed menu.</p>
</div>

### 1. Consultation and skin assessment

Your doctor examines skin type, barrier condition, degree of congestion, and any active inflammation. Recent isotretinoin use, recent peels or laser, and any history of cold sores are checked at this point. Fitzpatrick skin type is noted because it informs abrasion grade.

### 2. Cleansing and preparation

Makeup, sunscreen and surface sebum are removed. The eyes are protected. Areas to be avoided, such as inflamed lesions or open skin, are identified and mapped.

### 3. Serum selection

The serum is matched to the primary concern of the session — hydration, brightening or clarifying. The selection can be changed between sessions as the skin changes, which is the main advantage of the platform over a fixed-formula facial.

### 4. Dermalinfusion passes

The handpiece is worked across the treatment area in overlapping passes. Most patients describe a firm suction with mild scratchiness. Congested zones such as the nose may receive additional attention at a lower speed.

### 5. Aftercare and sunscreen

Skin is calmed, hydrated and protected with sunscreen before you leave. Your doctor confirms the interval to your next session and what to avoid over the following 48 hours.

## Who may be suitable for SilkPeel Dermalinfusion?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>SilkPeel Dermalinfusion may suit adults with congested, dull or dehydrated skin who want visible conditioning without peeling or social downtime.</strong> It is frequently chosen by patients who cannot accommodate recovery time and by patients being prepared for, or maintained between, more intensive treatments.</p>

<li>Dull or rough texture, with or without makeup sitting unevenly.</li>

<li>Patients in maintenance phase between laser or peel courses.</li>
<li>Patients preparing for an event within the coming week.</li>

## Who may not be suitable?

Suitability is assessed in person. Treatment is generally deferred or avoided in the following situations.

<ul class="clean">
<li>Active inflamed, pustular or cystic acne over the intended treatment area.</li>
<li>Active skin infection, including cold sores, impetigo or fungal infection.</li>
<li>Eczema, psoriasis or rosacea in an active flare, or a visibly compromised skin barrier.</li>
<li>Open lesions, wounds or recently biopsied skin.</li>
<li>Oral isotretinoin within the preceding 6 months, or a chemical peel, laser or energy-based treatment within the preceding 2 weeks.</li>
<li>Recent significant sun exposure or sunburn on the treatment area.</li>
<li>A history of keloid or hypertrophic scarring, which warrants a more cautious approach.</li>
<li>Pregnancy or breastfeeding, depending on the serum selected.</li>
</ul>

## Why does the serum choice matter?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>The serum is what converts a general exfoliation into a targeted treatment, because it is delivered at the exact moment the skin barrier is thinnest.</strong> Hydrating formulations typically centre on hyaluronic acid, brightening formulations on tyrosinase-inhibiting actives, and clarifying formulations on salicylic acid for congestion.</p>
</div>

One brightening active studied specifically with this delivery method is decapeptide-12, an oligopeptide that inhibits tyrosinase, the enzyme controlling the first steps of melanin synthesis. Laboratory work by Abu Ubeid and colleagues found it to be a considerably more potent tyrosinase inhibitor than hydroquinone in vitro, without the melanocyte toxicity associated with hydroquinone. Bhatia and Jimenez subsequently reported a case in *Journal of Drugs in Dermatology* in which a Fitzpatrick type IV patient with post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation was treated with four dermalinfusion sessions two weeks apart alongside daily home application and sun protection.

## How does SilkPeel fit into a treatment plan?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>At Klinik Dr Diana, SilkPeel Dermalinfusion is usually positioned as a maintenance or adjunctive treatment rather than as the primary intervention for a diagnosed skin condition.</strong> It conditions the surface, supports the result of deeper treatments, and gives patients something safe to do in the interval between them.</p>
