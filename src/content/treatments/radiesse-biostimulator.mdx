## What is Radiesse biostimulator treatment?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>Radiesse is an injectable calcium hydroxylapatite (CaHA) treatment that may support facial contour, firmness and gradual collagen stimulation.</strong> Depending on the technique, it can be used as a structural filler or as a collagen biostimulator for selected skin-quality and firmness concerns.</p>
</div>

Radiesse is different from a simple hydration booster. It does not mainly work by adding water to the skin. Instead, CaHA microspheres act as a scaffold that can support collagen remodelling over time, while the gel carrier may provide an earlier contour or support effect depending on how the product is placed.

## What you need to know

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>Radiesse can be helpful when the goal matches what CaHA can realistically do: support structure, improve selected contours and stimulate gradual collagen remodelling.</strong> It is not suitable for every skin concern and should not be chosen without assessment.</p>
</div>

<ul class="clean">
<li>Radiesse is made of calcium hydroxylapatite (CaHA) microspheres suspended in a gel carrier.</li>
<li>It can behave as a filler, a biostimulator, or both, depending on technique.</li>
<li>Some effect may be seen early, while collagen-related changes develop gradually.</li>
<li>It is not a hydration booster and is not primarily for pigmentation or active acne.</li>
<li>It is not dissolved with hyaluronidase like many hyaluronic acid fillers.</li>
<li>Correct placement, patient selection and injector experience are important for safety.</li>
</ul>

## Why is Radiesse called a biostimulator?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>Radiesse is called a biostimulator because CaHA can encourage the skin to produce new collagen over time.</strong> This collagen response may support firmness, elasticity and skin structure in suitable patients.</p>
</div>

This is different from a standard filler-only effect. A typical filler adds volume by occupying space. A biostimulator also aims to improve the tissue environment gradually by encouraging collagen remodelling.

The word "stimulator" should still be understood carefully. It describes the mechanism, not a guarantee. Results vary depending on skin quality, age, placement, dilution, technique and biological response.

## How does Radiesse work?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>Radiesse works through a dual mechanism: an early support effect from the gel carrier and a gradual biostimulatory effect from CaHA microspheres.</strong> Over time, the body gradually replaces the carrier while collagen remodelling may continue around the CaHA scaffold.</p>
</div>

How Radiesse behaves depends on technique. When used more structurally, it may support contour or deeper folds. When diluted or hyperdiluted, it is usually used more for collagen support, skin firmness and texture across a broader area.

## Who may be suitable for Radiesse?

<div class="answer">
<p><strong>Radiesse may suit selected adults who need contour support, facial structure refinement or gradual collagen-supporting treatment, provided their anatomy and medical history are suitable.</strong> It is not a one-size-fits-all injectable.</p>
</div>

Radiesse may be considered for patients who understand that the treatment must be planned according to anatomy and that collagen-related changes take time. Patients who want hydration only, very soft lip enhancement, tear-trough correction, active acne care or pigmentation treatment may need different options.

## Who may not be suitable?

Radiesse may not be suitable for patients who are pregnant or breastfeeding, have active infection or inflammation in the treatment area, have certain allergies or medical conditions, or have a treatment goal that would be safer or more appropriate with another product.

It may also be unsuitable when a patient wants a reversible hyaluronic acid filler, a hydration booster, or a treatment for concerns Radiesse does not primarily address.

## What results can you realistically expect?

</div>

Results are not permanent and cannot be guaranteed. Maintenance depends on the treated area, amount used, technique, metabolism, lifestyle and how your tissue responds.

## What are the possible side effects?

Common short-term effects may include swelling, bruising, redness, tenderness, itching, injection marks or temporary firmness. Lumps or nodules can occur, especially if product placement, technique or aftercare is not suitable.

Rare but serious filler-related complications can include infection, vascular compromise, skin injury, scarring or vision-related complications. Seek urgent medical attention if you develop severe pain, skin colour changes, blistering, worsening swelling, visual symptoms or symptoms that feel unusual after injection.
