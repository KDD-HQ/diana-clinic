## What Is Mounjaro (Tirzepatide)?

Mounjaro is the brand name for a medicine called tirzepatide, given as a once-a-week injection under the skin. It was developed first to treat type 2 diabetes, and it has also become well known for helping some people lose weight.

In simple terms, it is a prescription medicine, not a supplement and not a cosmetic "slimming jab." It works on the body's own hormone system to reduce hunger and help control blood sugar, which is why it can only be used safely with a doctor's guidance.

---

## How Does Mounjaro Work for Weight Loss?

Mounjaro works by copying two natural gut hormones called GLP-1 and GIP that your body normally releases after you eat. These hormones tell your brain that you are full, slow down how quickly your stomach empties, and help your body handle blood sugar and insulin.

The result is that many people feel less hungry, feel full sooner, and naturally eat less. Because Mounjaro acts on two hormone pathways at once, it is sometimes called a "dual agonist," while older medicines like Ozempic and Wegovy, which contain semaglutide, act on just one pathway called GLP-1.

Working on two pathways is part of why tirzepatide produced strong results in studies, but it does not mean it is the right choice for everyone.

## Is Mounjaro Approved and Legal in Malaysia?

Yes. Tirzepatide, the medicine in Mounjaro, is registered with Malaysia's National Pharmaceutical Regulatory Agency and is classed as a prescription-only medicine. Its main approved use is for type 2 diabetes, and doctors may also use it to support weight management in suitable patients after assessment.

Being prescription-only means it should only be obtained through a licensed doctor, clinic, or pharmacy. It should not be bought from social media sellers, unverified websites, or anyone who is not a registered medical provider.

This rule exists for your safety because the medicine needs proper screening, correct storage, and medical follow-up. Patients and clinics can confirm a product's registration status through the NPRA's official online database.

## Who May Be Suitable for Mounjaro?

Mounjaro may be considered for adults who are living with obesity, or who are overweight and also have a weight-related health problem, when diet and exercise alone have not been enough. This should only happen after a doctor confirms it is appropriate.

It is not meant for people who simply want to lose a few kilos quickly for an event or for appearance reasons.

A doctor will usually look at your weight and BMI, your medical history, any other conditions or medicines, and your overall health goals before deciding whether the treatment is suitable. The right decision is individual, which is why a face-to-face assessment matters more than a general rule from the internet.

## Who Should Not Use Mounjaro?

Some people should not use Mounjaro, and others need extra caution. It is not suitable for everyone, and only a doctor can confirm whether it is safe for you.

Mounjaro should generally not be used by people who:

* Have a personal or family history of a type of thyroid cancer called medullary thyroid carcinoma
* Have a genetic condition called Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 2
* Have had a serious allergic reaction to tirzepatide
* Have type 1 diabetes, as it is not approved for this

Extra caution and a careful doctor's review are needed for people who:

* Have had pancreatitis, which means inflammation of the pancreas
* Have had gallbladder problems
* Have severe stomach or digestion problems, such as severe gastroparesis
* Have kidney problems, especially if they become dehydrated from vomiting or diarrhoea
* Are pregnant, breastfeeding, or planning a pregnancy
* Take other medicines for diabetes such as insulin or sulfonylureas, which can raise the risk of low blood sugar

This is not a complete list, which is exactly why a medical screening is needed before starting.

## How Much Weight Can You Expect to Lose?

In clinical studies, people lost an average of about 15% to 21% of their body weight over roughly 72 weeks, depending on the dose, alongside lifestyle changes. For someone weighing 90 kg, that would be a rough average of around 14 to 19 kg.

However, this is an average from studies, not a promise for any individual.

Real-life results vary a lot. How much weight you lose depends on your starting weight, your dose, how your body responds, your diet and activity, and whether you can stay on treatment. Some people lose more, some lose less.

Mounjaro is a tool that works best alongside healthy eating and movement, not a replacement for them.

## What Are the Side Effects and Risks?

The most common side effects are stomach-related. These may include nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, reduced appetite, burping, or wind.

These side effects are usually worst in the first few weeks or after a dose increase, and often settle as the body adjusts. To reduce them, treatment is usually started at a low dose and increased slowly under the doctor's guidance.

Less common but more serious risks can include inflammation of the pancreas, gallbladder problems, dehydration that can affect the kidneys, low blood sugar when combined with certain diabetes medicines, and allergic reactions.

Because Mounjaro slows stomach emptying, you should also tell any doctor or anaesthetist before surgery or a procedure with sedation.

You should get urgent medical help for symptoms such as:

* Severe stomach pain that will not go away
* Signs of a serious allergic reaction
* A lump or swelling in the neck
* Severe vomiting or diarrhoea with dehydration
* Symptoms of very low blood sugar if you are also using diabetes medication

## Do You Keep the Weight Off After Stopping?

Not automatically. Many people regain a significant part of the weight after stopping the medicine, especially if lifestyle habits have not changed.

This is because the medicine reduces appetite while you are taking it. Once it stops, appetite can return.

This is why doctors treat weight management as a long-term plan rather than a short course. Eating habits, physical activity, sleep, and follow-up all matter for keeping results. Your doctor should discuss what happens during and after treatment before you start.
