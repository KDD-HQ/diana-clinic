## What Clues Can Help You Tell Pigmentation Types Apart?

The most useful clues are pattern, location, trigger, and behaviour, though these guide understanding rather than confirm a diagnosis. Broad symmetrical patches suggest melasma; marks where acne or injury occurred suggest PIH; separate spots on sun-exposed skin suggest sun spots; small lifelong spots that darken in sun suggest freckles. Pigmentation that recurs or flares suggests melasma, while marks that slowly fade suggest PIH. [1,3,4,5]

## Why Is It So Hard to Diagnose Pigmentation Yourself?

It is hard to self-diagnose because different types look similar, occur together, and sit at different depths. **Surface colour alone cannot show whether pigment is mainly epidermal, dermal, or mixed**, and depth affects both treatment choice and outcome. This is why copying a treatment that worked for someone else, or following a generic online recommendation, often leads to disappointment. [1,2,3,8]

## What If You Have More Than One Type at the Same Time? Mixed or Overlapping Concerns

A **mixed pattern** is common, for example melasma, old acne PIH, and a few sun spots together. This is one of the strongest reasons assessment matters: a single treatment rarely suits all the overlapping concerns equally, and one aimed at one type can worsen another. [1,2,3,4,5]

## How Does a Doctor Confirm the Type, and What Can Go Wrong Without It?

A doctor confirms the type by combining history with clinical examination, sometimes using a Wood lamp or dermoscope to estimate pigment depth. Diagnosis rests on the full picture, not one feature. Without this, the risks include:

* **Worsening pigmentation**, because aggressive or heat-based treatment can worsen melasma or trigger PIH. [3,6,7]
* **PIH after irritation or aggressive procedures**, especially in darker skin. [6,7]
* **Ineffective treatment** if the trigger, such as active acne or sun exposure, is not controlled. [3,11]
* **Missing a suspicious or changing lesion**, so changing spots should be checked using ABCDE. [12]

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I figure out my pigmentation type on my own?

You can gather clues from pattern, location, history, and triggers, but you cannot reliably confirm the type yourself because pigmentation types can look alike, coexist, and vary by depth. [1,2,3,8]

### Can I have more than one type at the same time?

Yes. A mixed pattern such as melasma with PIH or sun spots is common. [1,2,3,4]

### Why did my previous treatment not work?

Often the treatment did not match the actual type, or more than one type was present. [1,3,4,5,11]

### Does the depth of the pigment matter?

Yes. Epidermal pigment is generally more accessible, while dermal or mixed pigment can be more persistent. [1,3,8]

### Should I start treatment before getting it assessed?

It is safer to confirm the type first, as treating by appearance risks worsening pigmentation, especially in darker skin. [6,7,11]

### Is a Wood lamp or dermoscope examination painful?

No. These are non-invasive tools that help evaluate the extent and depth of pigment. [8,9]
